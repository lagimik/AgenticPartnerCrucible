# Anatomy of a Revenue-Generating Partner Offer

A durable pattern for turning a one-time, low-risk customer trial into a
packaged, repeatable Microsoft Marketplace/Partner Center offer with a
defined revenue structure — illustrated through Microsoft's "Copilot in
30" SMB offer template and its enterprise counterpart, the "Copilot
Envisioning & POC" funded engagement, but applicable to any trial-to-
managed-service partner motion.

## The Core Idea

Instead of scoping every engagement from scratch, a partner defines a
customer journey once — activities, timeline, deliverables — and runs it
repeatably across many customers. Publishing that journey as a Marketplace
offer turns expertise into a discoverable, scalable storefront rather than
a series of bespoke projects. Partner Center supports pairing a
professional-service offer (the bounded trial journey) with a
managed-service offer (the ongoing relationship that follows), so a single
published offer can carry both.

## Five-Stage Offer Structure

A repeatable offer journey breaks into five stages, each producing a
concrete output the customer sponsor can see:

1. **Identify** — build a prioritized target list from account-level
   signals (eligibility, whitespace, usage), confirm fit, and launch an
   acquisition campaign. The partner should experience the product
   internally first ("Customer Zero") before leading customers through it.
2. **Plan** — confirm a named customer sponsor and admin, agree success
   measures, and verify technical/compliance readiness before committing
   resources.
3. **Activate** — transact the trial/offer, configure renewal/billing
   settings up front (so conversion is the default path, not an
   afterthought), complete setup, and run a kickoff.
4. **Experience** — a structured cadence (e.g., weekly scenarios) that
   builds real usage and captures proof points in the customer's own
   words, with a mid-journey analytics checkpoint to re-engage low-activity
   users before the conversion decision point.
5. **Convert** — an outcome/ROI review that confirms the paid offer, and
   opens the next expansion/consumption conversation rather than treating
   conversion as the end of the engagement.

## Revenue Anatomy: Three Stacking Streams

A single converted engagement typically stacks multiple revenue sources
rather than relying on one:

- **Program incentives** (e.g., CSP incentive percentages) earned on every
  paid unit from the moment of conversion, and growing again at each
  expansion tier.
- **Deployment/adoption funding** (e.g., Microsoft Commercial Incentives
  funding) unlocked when the partner leads the conversion to a target
  tier, funding the deployment work that makes the expansion durable.
- **Managed services** — the recurring, largest, and most durable revenue
  share, sold as the "Day 31 and beyond" standing engagement (adoption
  management, license/expansion management, build-out work, governance,
  and quarterly value reviews) rather than a one-off project.

Treat published incentive/funding figures as illustrative and
time-bound — confirm current rates, funding availability, and eligibility
directly in Partner Center before quoting them to a customer or using them
in a business case.

## Prioritization Signal

Account-level reporting (e.g., Microsoft's AI Business Solutions &
Security Insights dashboard) surfaces eligibility, seat whitespace, and
adoption signals across a partner's existing managed base — letting the
partner target the accounts most likely to convert first, rather than the
first accounts to respond to a campaign.

## Why It Matters for Partners

- Provides a transferable offer-design template: any trial-eligible
  Microsoft product (not only Copilot) can be wrapped in this
  Identify→Plan→Activate→Experience→Convert structure and published as a
  paired professional-service + managed-service Marketplace offer.
- Reframes "the trial" as the cheapest part of the motion — the durable
  revenue is in the managed-service relationship the trial is designed to
  set up, which should be scoped and priced before the trial even starts.
- Gives partner practice leads a concrete worked example for building
  their own revenue model slide: multiple incentive streams stacking on
  top of the base engagement, rather than a single commission line.
