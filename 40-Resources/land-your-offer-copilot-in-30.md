# Land Your Offer - Copilot in 30 (Part 1 of Partner Offer Anatomy Series)

Microsoft Partner Community Blog walkthrough (2026-09-18, Part 1 of an
"anatomy of a revenue-generating partner offer" series) for **Copilot in
30**: a $0, 25-seat, 30-day Microsoft 365 Copilot Business CSP trial for
SMB customers (≤300 users, Microsoft 365 Business Basic/Standard/Premium,
no paid Copilot today), transactable through 31 December 2026, packaged as
a structured partner-led Identify→Plan→Activate→Experience→Convert
journey with a Day‑31-and-beyond managed-services follow-on. Includes a
full week-by-week activity/deliverable table and a worked revenue model
stacking CSP incentives, Frontier Accelerate deployment funding, and
managed-services revenue.

## Why It Matters for Partners

- A ready-to-publish Marketplace/Partner Center offer template partners
  can adapt directly — professional-service offer type for the 30-day
  trial journey, paired with a managed-service offer for the ongoing
  relationship — reducing offer-design time to near zero for SMB Copilot
  practices.
- Names the specific CSP New Commerce product/SKU (CFQ7TTC0MM8R · SKU
  006Z) and the exact eligibility rule (≤300 users, no paid Copilot, one
  trial per customer), so partners can validate pipeline fit quickly
  against their existing account base.
- Surfaces a prioritization tool most partners underuse: Partner Center's
  AI Business Solutions & Security Insights (ASPX) growth-insights
  reporting for account-level seat whitespace, eligibility, and adoption
  signals, plus a referenced companion Power BI scoring dashboard.
- Spells out a concrete, stacked revenue model (CSP incentive at 25 and 50
  seats, $2.5K Frontier Accelerate deployment funding at the 50-seat
  conversion, and ongoing managed services) useful as a template for a
  partner's own business case — with an explicit caveat that figures are
  illustrative estimates to confirm in Partner Center before quoting.
- Explicitly positions Day 30 conversion as the start, not the end, of the
  customer relationship — a reusable argument for selling managed
  services rather than one-off deployment projects.
