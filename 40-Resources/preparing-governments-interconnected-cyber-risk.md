# Preparing Governments for an Era of Interconnected Cyber Risk

Microsoft On the Issues post (2026-10-01) presenting MDDR 2026 government-
sector findings — governments are the most-impacted sector (27% of observed
activity) and the most frequently targeted for nation-state activity — and
five resilience priorities: preparing for faster attacker timelines,
building security into the AI ecosystem, planning for incidents to spread
across organizational/sectoral/national boundaries, enabling bidirectional
public-private information sharing, and preparing essential services to
operate through disruption via cross-sector tabletop exercises.

## Why It Matters for Partners

- Gives partners pursuing public-sector and SLED (state/local/education)
  security engagements a citable, dated (2026) Microsoft data point —
  government as the single most cyber-impacted sector and top nation-state
  target — for opening conversations and justifying resilience investment.
- The identity-centric statistics (phishing up from 7% to 23% of intrusions
  year over year; 52.2% of valid-account intrusions produce further
  credential theft) support leading public-sector security proposals with
  identity/Zero Trust controls rather than perimeter-only hardening.
- Priority 4 (bidirectional information sharing) and priority 5
  (cross-sector tabletop exercises, shared-service models) describe
  concrete, deliverable partner engagements: facilitated exercises,
  shared-service security operations for resource-constrained local
  governments, and information-sharing program design — not just technology
  deployment.
- Priority 2 ("build security into the AI ecosystem" as a resilience
  issue, not a separate agenda) gives partners language to connect AI
  governance/security engagements directly to existing critical-
  infrastructure and national-resilience mandates customers already fund.
- The SLED/critical-infrastructure attack-distribution breakdown (research
  and academia 38%, transportation 22%, government agencies 13%,
  communications 11%) helps partners prioritize which public-sector
  sub-verticals carry the highest near-term threat exposure.
