Hosting and hybrid cloud partners are currently navigating significant infrastructure transitions. In a May blog, Microsoft explored why changes in virtualization licensing, rising infrastructure costs, and evolving customer expectations are creating both pressure and opportunity for hosting businesses.

Now the conversation shifts from understanding the opportunity to taking action. It is about evolution without disruption: preserving what works, modernizing where it matters, and growing into higher-value services with Microsoft Adaptive Cloud.

That is why Microsoft created the new e-book, Navigating the Next Era of Hosting. It is a practical guide for hosting partners looking to protect margins, retain customer trust, and define a clear modernization path across Azure, hybrid, and AI-ready scenarios.

From awareness to action

Hosting partners are already fielding more complex customer conversations. Customers want flexibility across on-premises, edge, partner datacenter, and public cloud environments. They want consistent management, built-in security, stronger governance, and clear modernization options without being forced into a single destination or timeline.

At the same time, many partners are reevaluating long-standing platform strategies. Licensing changes, evolving commercial models, and rising infrastructure costs are prompting new questions about margin predictability, platform control, and long-term differentiation.

This is where action matters. Partners that move early can shape the conversation with customers instead of reacting to it. You can explain what will stay consistent, where modernization can begin, and how customers can retain choice while preparing for what comes next.

A phased path for hosting modernization

The next era of hosting does not require an all-or-nothing move. The strongest path is phased and practical, allowing you to preserve what works, modernize where it matters, and grow into higher-value services at a pace that fits your business and your customers.

The new e-book gives hosting partners a practical transition path that can align to your customer base, platform strategy, and business priorities.

In the first phase, stabilize and build customer confidence. This means maintaining customer trust, workload placement, and operational control while creating more visibility across the environments you already manage. It also means establishing policy and governance across environments and reassuring customers that modernization can happen without forcing immediate change.

In the second phase, modernize the platform at your pace. For many partners, that means using Azure Arc to bring a unified operating layer to existing environments, introducing Azure Local as a modern, Azure-integrated platform for partner-operated infrastructure, and applying Azure Native services where customers need advanced security, resilience, data, and application capabilities.

In the third phase, differentiate, expand, and grow. This is where hosting businesses can move beyond infrastructure rental and build recurring, services-led revenue around governance, security, optimization, modernization, and AI-ready workloads. It is also where partners can evolve from capacity-led hosting into Adaptive Cloud operations, delivering choice, consistency, and continuity while expanding long-term customer value.

The e-book also goes deeper into specific scenarios, including pay-as-you-go licensing for Windows Server and SQL Server through Azure Arc, Extended Security Updates for out-of-support workloads, migrating to Azure Local, and the move from Services Provider License Agreement (SPLA) models toward Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) services-led revenue.

Build momentum with Microsoft Adaptive Cloud

Microsoft Adaptive Cloud is the strategic foundation for this transition. With it, you can run workloads where they make the most sense, including in your datacenters, customer sites, edge locations, or public cloud, while managing them as one environment.

For hosting partners, Adaptive Cloud is commonly delivered through three complementary capabilities: Azure Arc extends Azure management and services to infrastructure running across on-premises, edge, and cloud environments; Azure Local gives partners a modern, Azure-integrated infrastructure path in partner-operated or hosted environments; Azure Native services create the growth layer.
