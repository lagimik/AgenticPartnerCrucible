## Reviewed Public Source

Article: What AI at work really costs.
Publisher: Microsoft Copilot Blog.
Author: Nicole Herskowitz.
Published: November 16, 2026.
URL: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/blog/2026/11/16/what-ai-at-work-really-costs/

The full article was accessible and reviewed. Microsoft commissioned and funded
the GigaOm research, ran the October 2026 testing and cost analysis, and says
GigaOm contributed to research design, validated methodology/results/cost
analysis, and co-developed findings. This is not an independently run,
unsponsored benchmark.

The article describes 37 unique prompts using a fictional company and common
underlying data, repeated runs, and modeled monthly frequencies: 100 tasks for
an everyday user and 140 for an advanced user, including 20 agentic workflows.
Products were Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT Enterprise, and Claude Enterprise.

| User workload | Copilot | ChatGPT | Claude |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Everyday | $30/month | $81/month | $205/month |
| Advanced | $96/month | $149/month | $299/month |

These are publisher-reported modeled monthly estimates using October 2026 list
prices, or assumed prices where unpublished, without negotiated discounts.
They are not customer invoices, binding quotes, realized savings, or a full
customer-specific implementation/operating-cost inventory.

Quality used human-authored prompt checklists: sources, people, facts/figures,
complete deliverables, and no unrequested actions. A run passed only if every
criterion was met. The article reports broadly comparable workload-level quality,
with task-level variation; it does not supply numerical pass rates here.

Microsoft attributes the modeled cost differences to subscription-included
everyday work, Work IQ context retrieval, and Auto model/effort routing.
Advanced agent workflows use usage-based Copilot Credits. Context efficiency
and model-routing examples are publisher explanations, not guarantees for
all workloads, tenants, model choices, or competing connectors.
